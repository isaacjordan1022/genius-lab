# Review one small learning cycle — about 10 minutes

Start with Aharon Zev’s first **five-activity chunk**, not a whole day or week. This is a parent walkthrough; your answers are test data, not evidence of Aharon’s abilities. The local pilot uses sample tickets and checkout.

The names now mean:

- **Weekly plan:** five daily lessons plus a larger weekly test.
- **Daily lesson:** three topic chunks, with 15 activities in total.
- **Chunk:** a short teaching experience followed by five varied activities. Each has two written responses, one matching activity, and two multiple-choice activities.

## 1. Check the map and the way back

Open [the dashboard](http://127.0.0.1:3100), then **Weekly plan** and the first available **Enter**. The daily plan previews its three chunks and learning objectives. Start or continue that daily lesson.

The lesson player has a **Lesson plan** link. Use it once, then return to the lesson. Can you tell the difference between the plan, the material, and the activities? Is the next action obvious? Your ticket balance should remain visible throughout the dashboard and lesson experience.

Earlier saved attempts are retained as their original version. To review the new structure, select the first current daily lesson from the weekly plan rather than reopening an old attempt in score history.

## 2. Review the first chunk’s teaching

Look at the vocabulary, passage, worked example, or subject card in front of you. Check that the important words are highlighted, paragraphs are short, and examples make the idea concrete. Decide whether Aharon can tell what to notice without reading a wall of text.

Choose **Try it**. During daily practice, **Revisit material** opens the relevant teaching without losing the answer draft. Try opening and closing it once. The weekly test is a separate, closed-reference check. Neither is timed in this pilot; timed assessment remains a design decision.

## 3. Try five varied activities

Complete this chunk’s five activities. Test the matching interaction with a mouse or touch, and try its keyboard alternative. For a written answer, judge whether the prompt asks for reasoning, evidence, an original connection, or a creative choice.

Leave for the daily plan or dashboard midway, then continue. Check that your answer draft and place survive. Submit the five activities and read the feedback. A **learning score of 4 out of 4 is not four tickets**: it measures that answer against the learning criteria. Tickets are a separate reward for passing the complete daily lesson.

Flag an explanation that merely repeats the answer, a matching activity that is awkward, or a prompt that is too easy, too vague, or too wordy. Explanations in this pilot are authored examples; personalized AI feedback and voice are still unconnected.

## 4. Check the handoff into the next chunk

After submission, responses lock and explanations appear. The two written responses await parent review. You can choose **Continue** while those reviews are pending; there is no grading bottleneck between chunks within the same day.

Stop here if you have enough feedback. You do not need to finish the remaining ten activities to judge this first cycle.

Optional: open the [parent area](http://127.0.0.1:3100/parent), local pilot PIN **2468**, in another tab. Grade the two submitted written responses by selecting only the criteria actually demonstrated. Review is available as soon as a chunk is submitted. Completing all three chunks produces **six written responses** for review in that daily lesson.

The next daily lesson and its reward require all 15 activities to be submitted and graded, with at least **54/60 learning points (90%)**. A lower final score offers a fresh form. Do not inflate a score to unlock play; a frustrating recovery path is useful feedback. The weekly test has 24 activities and uses its own denominator of 96 points.

## 5. Compare the style guide

Open the [style lab](http://127.0.0.1:3100/style-lab). The selected fonts are **DynaPuff + Lexend**. Compare:

- **Three light backgrounds:** Soft cream, Cloud sage, and Pearl gray. The motifs should feel faint and small; reading should stay calm.
- **Type hierarchy and weights:** DynaPuff 400 or 500 for normal headings; every interface label is at least 18px; Lexend 400 for reading, 500 for supporting labels, and 600 for questions/actions.
- **Color families and ticket printing:** judge contrast, outlined headlines, depth, and the ticket balance at dashboard size.
- **Tick’s six moments:** idle, blink, greeting, thinking, printing, and thumbs-up. Tap a pose and decide whether it feels warm, clear, and restrained enough around learning.

## 6. Visit the Prize Room

Open the [Prize Room](http://127.0.0.1:3100/prize-room). All researched products are available directly, with clean product images, ticket costs, and progress toward each prize. Golden tickets represent family experiences. Filter electronics, sports, toys, or other categories; check that the room feels exciting and easy to explore.

There is no parent picker. Isaac manages products through this development conversation. The pilot conversion is 15 tickets per dollar, rounded up to the next ten tickets. A $200 item costs 3,000 tickets, or five base learning weeks at 600 tickets/week. This provisional economy still needs playtesting. Experience prices are configured separately.

Checkout spends the child's saved tickets once, creates a confirmed prize order and local parent notification, and records prize history. It does not buy a product from the retailer or send an external notification. Do not test checkout against the child's actual balance unless you intend to spend those pilot tickets; automated tests use a separate database.
